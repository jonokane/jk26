## Overview

The 8tracks first-time experience was the moment a new listener either fell for the product or drifted away. Over roughly three months, I led a redesign of the FTUX across iOS, Android, and web, with a clear job to do: engage new users immediately and capture enough taste signal to power more relevant playlist recommendations, which in turn supports retention.

## The challenge

A first run has to earn attention in seconds while quietly doing real work: reading a person's taste well enough to make the next recommendation feel personal. Too much friction and people bail; too little signal and the recommendations fall flat. Finding that balance across three platforms was the puzzle.

## What I did

I started from a shared team vision and treated it as a hypothesis rather than a spec. We validated and refined it with qualitative research, usability testing, and A/B testing along the way, so the shipped design was earned by evidence, not opinion. Once the interaction model held up, I partnered with our visual designer to bring it to a polished finish, then worked closely with engineering to build the updated experience consistently across platforms.

## Outcome

The redesigned first run shipped across iOS, Android, and web in Spring 2016. Because the team vision was treated as a hypothesis rather than a spec, every part of the flow that survived to launch had first been through qualitative research, usability testing, or an A/B test. The lesson that stayed with me was about restraint: a first run earns the right to ask for taste signal by giving something back immediately, and the fastest way to lose a new listener is to make the product feel like a form.
