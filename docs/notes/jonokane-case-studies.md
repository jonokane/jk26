## Amazon Key: In-Car Delivery

**Client:** Amazon
**Sector:** Automotive Tech, IoT
**Discipline:** Product Design, Product Strategy / Leadership
**Platform:** iOS, Android, Vehicle
**Role:** Design Lead (one of two), in-car delivery
**Timeframe:** Launched Spring 2018
**Duration:** 2+ years
**View the work:** amazon.com/key
**Confidentiality:** Public
**One-line impact:** Invented and shipped in-car package delivery for a brand-new Amazon program, from zero to a live product across iOS, Android, and connected vehicles.

### Overview
In-car delivery was, for me, the defining work of a long career. I helped a team of designers, product partners, and engineers invent an entirely new way to receive Amazon packages: securely, inside your own vehicle. We shipped in-home delivery in Fall 2017 and followed with in-car in Spring 2018. I served as one of two design leads, focused on the in-car experience, which meant equal parts inventing something that did not exist and crafting it to feel unmistakably Amazon.

### The challenge
A day-one product has no patterns to lean on. We had to solve three hard problems at once. First, explain a genuinely new concept: early testing showed that even users who had read the program overview did not understand that their car had to be parked at the delivery location. Second, earn trust: people consider their car a private space, and we were asking to open it. Third, work across a fragmented hardware reality: every vehicle maker implemented cloud connectivity differently, and none offered a public API, so there was no clean backend to build against.

### What I did
I owned the in-car setup experience end to end, and I carried the design language, patterns, and tone we had built for in-home over to the vehicle use case. To solve comprehension, I paired lightweight onboarding cards with explanatory copy on every single setup screen, because research told us plainly that some users skip the intro and still need to succeed. To build trust, I tuned the voice of every screen toward a responsible, careful brand, and designed delivery cards and notifications that reminded people exactly where to park and clearly signaled when their vehicle would be accessed. To handle the OEM fragmentation, I designed the flow around embedded webviews of each maker's sign-in, and accounted for eligibility edge cases like region and Prime status.

The process was relentlessly iterative and deeply cross-functional. We learned early to bring engineering into the room from the first sketch, because a beautiful idea that cannot be built is wasted motion. From aligned sketches we moved to high-fidelity flows, then validated the riskiest assumptions with our research lead through structured studies. Because the program was confidential, early research ran with a small internal sample, which pushed us to deliberately recruit across very different functions and levels to avoid designing for a single persona.

### Outcome
In-car delivery went live in April 2018 and iterated on weekly design sprints and roughly biweekly releases. Our north-star metric was customer happiness, tracked closely through two in-app feedback channels. What began as a strange, novel idea steadily became a normal, delightful way for people to receive their packages. The bigger lesson stayed with me: when you are inventing something with no precedent, clarity and trust are not polish, they are the product.

## 8tracks: First-Time User Experience

**Client:** 8tracks
**Sector:** Music Tech, Creative Tech
**Discipline:** Product Design, Product Management
**Platform:** iOS, Android, Web
**Role:** Design lead / design management
**Timeframe:** Launched Spring 2016
**Duration:** ~3 months
**View the work:** 8tracks.com
**Confidentiality:** Public
**One-line impact:** Rebuilt the first-time experience for a beloved music platform to learn user taste faster and feed better playlist recommendations from day one.

### Overview
The 8tracks first-time experience was the moment a new listener either fell for the product or drifted away. Over roughly three months, I led a redesign of the FTUX across iOS, Android, and web, with a clear job to do: engage new users immediately and capture enough taste signal to power more relevant playlist recommendations, which in turn supports retention.

### The challenge
A first run has to earn attention in seconds while quietly doing real work: reading a person's taste well enough to make the next recommendation feel personal. Too much friction and people bail; too little signal and the recommendations fall flat. Finding that balance across three platforms was the puzzle.

### What I did
I started from a shared team vision and treated it as a hypothesis rather than a spec. We validated and refined it with qualitative research, usability testing, and A/B testing along the way, so the shipped design was earned by evidence, not opinion. Once the interaction model held up, I partnered with our visual designer to bring it to a polished finish, then worked closely with engineering to build the updated experience consistently across platforms.
